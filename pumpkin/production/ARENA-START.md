## Contest

Use a bounded four-round allocation as a starting plan: 24 independent process proposals, 24 fresh adversarial reviews, 16 revised finalist proposals, 36 specialized finalist reviews. This totals 100 assignments, not 100 concurrent agents. Reallocate only to resolve a documented uncertainty; fewer strong candidates are preferable to redundant roles. Dispatch independent proposers with the same human brief and evidence, without previous proposals. Blind first-pass reviewers to proposer identities and prior verdicts. Give revision agents concrete findings after first-pass review. Keep a public evidence ledger.

Proposals must differ materially: reference-first art production, rapid mechanic experimentation, template adaptation, Studio-first authoring and combinations. Each proposes how to choose the core action, demonstrate social benefit, build convincing art and detect a weak result early. Require exact first three deliverables, who produces them, required tools, free foundations with inspected license/fit, tests and abandon/revise signals. No generic feature checklist or invented proprietary template.

Review criteria: fidelity to the user's fantasy; child-readable first action; intentional friend benefit; visual production feasibility; actual Studio evidence; speed to a meaningful product decision; reuse at larger scale without needless ceremony. Review the first-session route, pumpkin silhouettes/size states, animation, VFX timing, SFX, map composition, UI/mobile camera and the proposed integration order. Do not vote based on prose quality, popularity claims or number of agents agreeing.

Finalists must specify cheap, falsifiable Studio experiments and what result would eliminate their approach. Where safe tools are available, conduct the experiments and inspect captures/logs. If unavailable, choose only the next experiment to run; product-quality victory remains pending. A successful contest identifies the most useful tested approach for this brief, not a universally perfect method.
