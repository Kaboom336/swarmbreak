# Blind visual balance critique — R1

Evidence checked: only `READABLE-SHEET.jpg` and `concept-v2.1.png`, viewed directly. Scores are visual inference from still images. Actual player timing, traversal, collision, and Studio lighting are UNKNOWN.

## Independent scores

- **Where to go in 3 seconds: 7/10.** The portal view presents three named mouths: MAIN / CAMP at left, CART STATION centrally, CAVE at right. The main player view has a broad, continuous pale walkway rising toward the crystal hill; the cart view clearly shows rails on wood; the cave view shows a walkable pale path into a lantern-lit opening. These are readable route types. However, the portal camera is elevated, so its overview is more informative than a true player entry view. The main route bends behind a crest without a visible camp destination. At the junction, rails and walking pavement cross and merge visually; the far cart destination is hidden by the rise. The cave label and arch help, but the largest nearby crystals compete strongly with the entrance. The colored overhead plan clarifies topology but does not count as player-facing guidance.
- **Look quality and atmospheric resemblance: 5/10.** The slate-blue floating rock mass, blue night backdrop, cyan/magenta cliff clusters, warm path lights, ramps, and mine arch carry the reference's basic identity. The far view has a strong floating-island silhouette. Yet the concept has textured stone, faceted translucent crystal light, orange lantern pools, timber supports, railings, crates, banners, and distinct inhabited landmarks. Here crystals read as intensely bright flat neon solids; lamps read as yellow squares; terrain reads as repetitive block steps with black gaps. Sparse mine dressing and mostly bare, beige paths reduce atmosphere and scale. Cool night color is present, but warm illumination is too weak and localized to give the concept's inviting mine settlement character.

## Three priority criticisms

1. **Show destinations at the route decision.** MAIN / CAMP points toward an unlabeled hill path, and the cart ramp hides its endpoint. Give each mouth a visible destination silhouette or a strong repeated route marker beyond the first bend. Add a compact camp landmark visible from the main mouth and an unmistakable cart station/stop beyond the rail rise. Judge this from actual eye-height entry views.
2. **Reduce foreground crystal dominance and clarify the junction.** Giant cyan pillars and saturated pink peaks pull the eye away from the cave entrance and transport choices. Move or shrink the closest clusters, retain an open sightline through each mouth, and make walking pavement and rail crossings visibly separate with edge treatment and purposeful crossing details. Current rails are recognizable; their junction behavior is less clear.
3. **Build warm, inhabited mine material detail.** Replace yellow light squares with lantern housings and wider amber pools; add timber braces, rails/edge barriers, crates, and one convincing station structure. Introduce irregular slate faces and richer faceted crystals with restrained brightness. This would close more of the concept gap than adding more glowing clusters.

Lesson: When luminous decoration is brighter than the destination, reduce its foreground weight because attraction and navigation compete. When a route rises out of view, expose a destination landmark because a clear path alone does not explain its reward.
