# Independent blind visual review — R1

Evidence: inspected only `readable-r1-portal.png`, `readable-r1-main-eye.png`, `readable-r1-cart-eye.png`, `readable-r1-cave-eye.png`, and `readable-r1-far.png`. Portal image inspected first, without implementation or other documents. The three-second judgment is visual inference, **not a real user test**.

## Unlabeled portal: FAIL for knowing where to go in three seconds

The immediate, strongest attraction is the large cyan spiral portal. A new player could reasonably walk into it rather than start any of the routes behind it. Three possible routes are visible: a pale walkway climbing left beneath a blank green board, orange rails climbing near the center behind the portal, and a pale walkway passing through a cyan gate on the right toward a dark opening. The image does not communicate which route is the intended first action or what each route offers.

The left walkway is easiest to trace locally because its warm lamps and pale surface make a continuous line. Its destination is visually weak. The cart route has distinctive rails, but the portal uprights obscure its starting connection and the central yellow board is largely hidden. The right route has a distinctive cyan gate and dark tunnel, but its path disappears behind foreground portal geometry and other supports. The bright distant pink crystal is recognizable, but it cannot establish the relationship between these route choices by itself. Blank colored boards look like missing information, not directional cues.

## Other views

- **Main eye:** The pale path and lamps read clearly as walkable. The broad bend/junction offers a right branch and an uphill continuation without a visible reason to choose either. A huge blank green board occupies the upper left. The stepped rock mound ahead reads as a block pyramid; its dark inset is too small and weak to establish a destination.
- **Cart eye:** Rails clearly identify the cart route, and warm orange decking distinguishes it from walking paths. The huge yellow sign and two posts block the uphill view and much of the pink landmark. Its empty face offers no compensation for that obstruction. The cave entrance at right is clearer than the rail destination.
- **Cave eye:** Best local destination clarity. Pale walkway and successive warm lamps lead into a recognizable dark tunnel. The cyan gate pillar masks the left edge of the entrance from this angle. The surrounding empty blue space weakens the sense of entering a substantial cave.
- **Far:** The course is visibly interconnected, with a strong central rail climb and a tall pink terminal landmark. This overhead view reveals topology that the portal view does not. Many crossings and similarly lit branches still make the intended sequence ambiguous. The floating crystal island is more convincing than the ground-level mine environment.

## Visual look

The cool purple stone, pale wood, dark rail metal, warm lamps, and cyan/pink crystal palette are coherent. The cave mouth has useful shadow depth and repeated supports. However, much of the rock is regular square tiling and terrace steps, giving the mounds an engineered pyramid shape instead of fractured stone. A few faceted chunks near the cave show a more convincing direction; that variation is not yet carried across the terrain.

Crystals frame the underside well in the far view. At eye level, isolated floating crystal/stone pieces hang against a flat blue backdrop and read as decoration suspended in space. The tall pink crystal is a strong landmark, but signs and the portal conceal it in the critical starting views. Cyan portal glow competes with that destination, while warm lamps mark all routes equally. Ambient light is sufficiently bright to expose paths but flattens most rock volume; tunnel blacks are abrupt relative to the exterior.

## Highest-value changes

1. Expose all three route starts from the portal: move or shrink the portal uprights and oversized sign boards that interrupt sightlines.
2. Give each route one legible destination cue at its entrance; make the intended first route visually dominant. Blank boards cannot explain the choices.
3. Preserve a visible line toward the pink landmark from the rail entrance; lower the yellow board or place it beside the tracks.
4. Use a smaller number of larger, irregular rock masses around the cave and mounds. Break the stair-pyramid silhouettes before adding more decoration.
5. Connect hanging formations visually to a ceiling or intentionally present the scene as a floating crystal island. The current eye views do not establish an enclosing mine cave.

Lessons: When a foreground landmark is brightest, treat it as the implied action because players may stop there. When route signs cover destinations, move them beside paths because the obstruction outweighs their color cue. When an overhead layout looks readable, still check eye-level junctions because height hides conflicting crossings.
