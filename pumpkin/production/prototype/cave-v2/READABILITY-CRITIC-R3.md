# Independent blind visual review

Evidence: inspected only the five supplied saved images, portal first. Findings are visual inference; no player timing test, movement test, source inspection, or prior review.

## Portal route readability — PASS

By visual inference, a new player can identify the available routes within roughly three seconds. This does not establish which destination is the required first objective.

- Left: the broad pale rising path and green MAIN / CAMP sign make a clear walking route.
- Centre: the yellow CART STATION sign sits over the narrower connecting platform beside the brown rail approach. It is identifiable, though the sign-to-mouth relationship is less immediate than the other two.
- Right: the cyan CAVE sign and matching doorway frame mark a separate pale walking approach to the dark cave entrance.
- Landmarks: the giant magenta crystal gives the upper island a strong reference point; cyan marks the cave and portal; warm lamps trace the walkways.
- Continuity: pale decking separates routes from grey rock. The left ascent and centre rail ramp remain easy to follow. The cave approach continues behind the cyan frame, but its bend and the dark opening partially hide the next segment.
- Obscuration: signs and posts overlap parts of the junction; the rail ramp sits close to the cave sign, so the sign initially could seem to label the uphill rail. The cave frame resolves this ambiguity. The bright portal occupies the left foreground but does not hide the route choices.

## Look and route confirmation

- Lighting: warm path lamps contrast effectively with cold stone and cyan/magenta crystals. Pale paths are visible against the rocks. Some lamp faces are nearly featureless yellow, and cave shadows collapse to black, reducing depth and detail.
- Crystal framing: the tall crystal is a strong destination landmark. Hanging crystals make the floating island silhouette distinctive in the far view. At ground level the scene has much empty blue-grey space; the floating fragments read as decoration more than an enclosing cavern.
- Coherence: repeating stone, timber rails, lamps, crates, and the dark supported tunnel communicate a stylized floating mine island. The cyan rectangular gateway looks more technological than the mine construction, but its route-marking role is visually clear.
- Main eye: continuous broad path, lamps, and a visible upper bend support navigation. The oversized near sign is clipped at the top and its post consumes considerable view space. The end destination itself is not strongly distinguished from rock and crates.
- Cart eye: rails clearly lead uphill. Brown block objects sit in or beside the track and may read as obstacles rather than a usable cart station. This image alone does not establish where or how a player boards.
- Cave eye: cyan frame, pale path, dark mouth, and lamps continuing inside establish a clear entrance. The roof and pillars obscure much of the interior; illumination should reveal the first interior turn or stopping point more clearly.
- Far view: the path network, central rail, cave mass, and tall crystal form one coherent island. The rail and pale footpaths remain distinct. Black roof gaps and shadows make the cave structure look somewhat unfinished from above.

## Concrete remaining concerns

1. Move or orient the CAVE sign so it unmistakably belongs to the cyan doorway rather than the adjacent rail ascent.
2. Give CART STATION a visibly recognizable boarding point or cart; signs alone carry its function.
3. Reduce nearby sign/post bulk and reveal a little more cave interior detail without losing the dark entrance contrast.

Lesson: when several routes meet, match each sign physically to its entrance because colour and labels alone can leave overlapping route ownership ambiguous.
