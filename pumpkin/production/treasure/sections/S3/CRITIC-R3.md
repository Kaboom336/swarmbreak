**Decision: REVISIT.** The repeated timber frames and warm lantern rhythm establish a readable mine corridor. The darker interior against daylight is working. It still falls short of TARGET's grounded excavation, continuous floor craft, and purposeful mining history.

## Three biggest gaps

1. **The walking surface reads as overlapping construction sheets.** Middle and junction show large straight diagonal seams, lifted-looking plate edges, and scattered flat patches; entry and reverse have conspicuous green strips along the walls. Compared with TARGET's continuous earth and stone curb, these weaken both track-edge clarity and believable ground contact. Make the route one coherent earthen surface with deliberate, continuous edge treatment and restrained embedded wear.

2. **Mining story and structural contact are too thin.** The scene is dominated by repeated frames and lights; the small mushrooms and patches do not communicate a worksite. TARGET's cart, ore crates, rubble pockets, and stone timber feet make its supports and activity legible. Give a few recesses distinct mining groups, and make major timber feet visibly bear on rock or footings instead of disappearing into the wall/floor treatment.

3. **The two-way shortcut lacks a strong destination read.** Middle's left/forward arrows are visible, but they name no destinations; junction and far are dominated by a blank hanging board and broad daylight opening. Reverse presents another open end without an equally clear cue. The overhead open sky/vegetation at the junction also weakens the enclosed mine silhouette. Frame each exit as a deliberate portal and give the choice clear, consistent landmarks or readable destination signs.
