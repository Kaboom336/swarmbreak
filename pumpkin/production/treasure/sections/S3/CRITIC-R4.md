## Exactly three biggest remaining gaps

1. **Enclosure weakens at the junction.** Entry and reverse have a convincing supported corridor, but middle and junction show broad pale sky above the portal and exposed green strips against the rock bases. TARGET carries a heavier continuous rock ceiling and grounded wall edges. The overview is explicitly a cutaway, so its absent roof alone is not evidence of a missing runtime roof.
2. **Mining story and track definition remain thin.** The timber frames and lanterns work, but the visible floor reads as scattered flat brown patches and green discs; it does not establish a continuous track. No comparably legible ore cart, loaded crates, or worked mining pocket appears in these views. TARGET's cart, ore, rails, stone edging, and wet central channel make its purpose more specific. Current clutter is restrained, but much of it adds surface noise rather than mining meaning.
3. **Two numbered signs are readable, but the two route choices are less clear.** Middle explicitly shows EXIT 2 pointing left and EXIT 1 forward. At junction and far, one broad bright opening dominates; the cutaway shows the branching footprint more clearly than the player views do. The signs therefore communicate the choice better than the visible route geometry. Each arrow should lead the eye to its own unmistakable opening from the decision point.

Warm lantern faces and repeated supports are the strongest elements. Floor/edge warmth is weaker than the lamps themselves, and bright green base strips interrupt the grounded mine appearance. The scene is not overloaded with props.

**Runtime UNKNOWN:** collision and head clearance, whether both signed exits can actually be traversed, shortcut travel benefit, player-scale camera readability while moving, lighting in the live renderer, and performance. Static images cannot verify these.

**Lessons:** When evaluating enclosure, use player views because cutaways deliberately remove cover. When two routes must be chosen, show both openings at the decision point because signs alone leave the geometry ambiguous. When adding mining detail, use one recognizable work prop because scattered floor patches do not carry the same story.
