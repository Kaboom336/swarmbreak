# Independent retention critic

Evidence checked: BLIND-PACK.md only. All scores and predicted behavior below are INFERRED, untested. No source, other critic, implementation, Studio, or player evidence was inspected.

**Winner: Z, narrowly.** Short spoke returns preserve more time for steals, pet reveals, and visible progress. Choosing a retreat spoke could become a real recurring decision. That advantage is conditional: the mandated clockwise permit sequence currently restricts which treasure to pursue, and spokes need meaningfully different readable risks. Geometry alone does not establish choice.

X gives the clearest aspiration and easiest first reward. Its late game spends more time retracing cleared space; purchased speed makes that trip shorter without making it more interesting. Y adds turns and travel while obscuring home and the next threat. Its visual compactness does not pay the player back for that cost.

## Biggest failure mode

All three risk becoming fifteen compulsory banks punctuated by coin waits. Stronger pets, permits, and boots mostly service the next numbered tier; the pack supplies little reason to revisit an older tier or return after reaching fifteen. Cosmetic trails are a proposed reward, not evidence of replay motivation. Preserving access on a future mastery reset is sensible, but does not itself create a replay loop.

The exponential curve does not prove a long progression. With one common pet from zone n, the next permit costs exactly 60 seconds of that pet's income. A boots rank matching that zone costs 30 seconds of that income. Earlier pets and elapsed raid time reduce the remaining wait; a rare or epic changes affordability sharply. With proposed odds, expected income is 2.1 times common income for a single hatch, but individual outcomes vary greatly. Walking, opening, saving, collection cadence, and eight-pet replacement could dominate actual pacing; those times are UNKNOWN. Fifteen tiers may be consumed quickly or feel slow through friction, despite enormous displayed numbers.

Z's additional ring gates need clarification: charging an extra ring fee on top of per-zone permits would introduce another chore without a stated player benefit. Do not add that fee in the first test. Speed recommendations also risk becoming compulsory purchases if a lower-rank player cannot reliably finish an otherwise legal run.

## Concrete correction

Test Z with only the first five zones, explicit next-target guidance, and no extra ring fee. After each first bank, let the player choose between a repeat raid for a clearly previewed pet opportunity and the newly authorized zone. Give its two exits distinct visible tradeoffs, such as a short exposed spoke versus a longer sheltered spoke; both must remain viable on foot. Keep the existing permit formula only as a provisional tuning value.

Record first-bank time, voluntary repeat raids, choices between exits, time waiting for Coins, replacement friction, and whether players voluntarily start another run after the fifth bank. Ask returning players what they came back to do. Do not expand to fifteen zones until that small loop produces an observable reason to repeat. No numeric acceptance target can be justified from this pack alone.

## Lessons

- When income and prices double together, inspect time-to-afford because large numbers can hide nearly constant pacing.
- When travel grows, add a recurring decision because distance alone mainly adds elapsed time.
- When testing retention, observe voluntary repeat play because completing mandatory gates only demonstrates progression compliance.
