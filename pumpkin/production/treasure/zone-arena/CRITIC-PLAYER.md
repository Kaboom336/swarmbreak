**Winner: X.** “Grab the treasure, dodge the farmer, run back to your pet house” reads fastest. A crown at the far end gives me somewhere obvious to want to reach. Someone dropping a crown and another player rescuing it could make a readable silent clip, provided the cargo stays visually obvious.

**Biggest risk: the return becomes empty running.** Fifteen colour changes do not make fifteen interesting escapes. Once I learn that most guardians ignore my cargo, the deepest run may feel like waiting for my speed upgrade to finish the trip. One shared early treasure also risks making my first experience watching someone else play.

**Concrete correction for X:** make one short first-run slice before expanding: home, three visibly different treasure bays, one farmer bait moment, one return fork, and the bank-to-pet reveal. Test whether a silent phone clip clearly shows the stolen item, approaching danger, safe line and pet payoff. Keep the return fork as a brief choice with visible, precommitted danger; preserve the guaranteed walking bypass. If that slice feels empty, fix its escape before producing the remaining sections. Test the eight-player first pickup separately; the shared item may need faster early refill or more visible alternate objectives.

Y's turns make me ask where home is while giving me more distance to run. Its concrete correction is a continuous visible home-direction guide and one unmistakable return landmark per court, then test whether the extra travel earns its time. The proposal does not establish that yet.

Z has the best potential group-escape shot: friends sprinting toward pets along spokes. But “which numbered arc can I steal from?” interrupts that simplicity. Its concrete correction is to highlight exactly one next eligible treasure and its spoke from home, with a visible previous-bank requirement at the treasure. Test whether I can find it without reading a map or opening menus.

Lessons: When progression is a long path, make the next reward and home direction visually obvious because a phone viewer has little time to decode them. When banking is the payoff, test the complete steal-to-pet loop because a good chase alone does not prove another run is appealing.
