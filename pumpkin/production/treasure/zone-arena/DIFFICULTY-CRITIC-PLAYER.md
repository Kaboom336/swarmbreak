**Winner: X.** Its visible home, visible far prize and straight escape make both success and failure easiest to understand on a phone. A clip should show the treasure, readable cue, deliberate dodge and home crossing in one continuous sequence. Do not depend on captions to explain it.

**Strongest objection:** the document promises hard encounters, but a 696-stud late return can still become mostly running after one good dodge. The rarity/reward curve does not turn dead time into difficulty. One shared early item also risks making my first 30 seconds a queue.

**Concrete correction:** prototype zones 1–3 with the real default phone camera, then test a representative zone 8 and zone 15 route on paper or in a later authorized playable. Each needs an observable mistake and an immediate lesson: “I dashed before the locked tell,” “I rushed the landing,” or “I picked the wrong seal opening.” Place the three encounter sockets so the return has decisions separated by recovery, not empty jogging. Instrument treasure waiting separately from mechanical failure. If the first item is unavailable, give another readable starter opportunity without forcing a queue.

The new/practiced target gap is promising, especially 40/74% at zone 8 and 10/46% at zone 15. Those are **INFERRED tuning targets**, not evidence the difficulty works. Zone 15 should be a desirable optional mastery challenge; losing unbanked cargo must never remove my home income. Three-second local empty retries are essential because a 10% first-try target becomes irritating if every retry requires another long approach.

Fairness conditions to retain: visible 1.2-second cues from the player's actual view, locked attacks, a timing-based walking option, no rival collision tricks, no surprise pileup, and upgrades that help approach/escape without skipping the new decision. Keepers becoming faster should not quietly shorten these cues.

Lesson: When the intended audience watches short clips, make the threat, mistake and recovery legible in one camera sequence, because a long route alone does not show skill.
