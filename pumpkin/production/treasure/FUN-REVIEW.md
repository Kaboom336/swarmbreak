## Why our loops feel unfun

The diagnosis below is **INFERRED from documents plus Zion's verdict**, not a claim to have observed players quitting.

| Problem | Evidence in our designs | Why it matters / change |
|---|---|---|
| We keep designing tests of obedience. | Crown/zone conditions, parry windows, lane tells, carry holds and timed returns put the guardian's schedule first. | A different warning is not a different fantasy. Give the thief something mischievous to do immediately. |
| Rules outnumber interesting choices. | GDD includes first-hit retention, first-pickup shield, shared mercy, drop/settle/Grab, idle-drop and the item deadline, before plot/reveal/income. | Players can spend their attention interpreting exceptions. Keep correctness behind the scenes; do not make understanding that machinery the entertainment. |
| “Dodge and walk home” is mostly defensive. | PACIFIST's wall bait and route cutback are the main expressive acts; raid lanes add another threat to cross. | The level and NPC author most surprises. A thief should also initiate a trick, a stunt, a betrayal or a rescue. |
| Multiplayer is narrower than it sounds. | Bump/Grab already exists; later zone proposals reduce rivalry to racing and loose-cargo recovery. HEIST disables active interference and gives helpers no shared reward. | This is not missing multiplayer code. It is too few distinct stories that another player can cause. A friend should change the plan, not merely delay the same run. |
| The treasure behaves like a delivery token. | Acorn → escape → place → open → pet → Coins → Feed/unlock. The exciting object becomes another familiar collection pipeline. | Let the stolen object alter play while it is stolen. Its size, motion, disguise or personality should create the fun before a reward panel appears. |
| The payoff is fragmented and the brag is weak. | Placement, reveal, collection and upgrades distribute the reward across steps; later islands repeat a similar acquisition structure. | “I got the next rate” is weaker than “we stole his whole bed” or “look at everything in my ball.” Show the real haul, then its real value. |
| More polish cannot choose the joke for us. | SIMPLIFY reduces repeated labels and camera interruptions. Pets/coins add persistence; raid adds activity. | Smoother presentation can remove friction. It cannot establish that the repeated action itself is enjoyable. Stop adding layers to rescue a loop Zion dislikes. |

Keep durable ownership and correct awards behind the scenes. **Stop presenting rule coverage as a fun hook.** These six alternatives reopen prior interaction constraints for discussion; none silently changes the existing GDD, saves, combat policy or shipped behavior.

### What makes a clip, rather than just a feature list

The TikTok research includes captions, checked frame sheets and some creator advice; several spoken claims/audio details could not be recovered. It supports **design hypotheses**, not a formula proving virality.

| Clip ingredient | Playable moment we should seek |
|---|---|
| Chaos + other-player stealing | One coveted thing visibly changes hands; the next player can reverse the outcome. |
| Absurd character | A king stays asleep while his bed is being stolen; the character is involved in the action, not just a funny shop portrait. |
| Huge numbers | A visibly huge haul bursts into its actual earned payout. Readable magnitude follows real progress; no made-up jackpot. |
| Near-miss escape | Everyone understands what is about to go wrong before the last-second save. |
| Trolling | A player deliberately causes a reversible surprise, such as passing the screaming gem; quick retaliation is possible. |
| Big reveal | A “treasure” grows legs, or a junk sphere opens into everything actually collected. |

**Muted-phone test for a later proof:** can a stranger see desire → player-caused surprise → outcome without reading a rules panel? A funny thumbnail alone does not pass. Every concept also needs a second run that differs because of a player choice, not just another rarity roll.

## Six bold concepts

Each pitch has exactly five lines: hook, first-minute target, clip, distinction and smallest future proof. These are alternatives, not six systems to combine. Times and fun are hypotheses.

### 1. Screaming Diamond
- **Hook:** Steal a diamond that screams its carrier's name, then throw it to redirect the furious guardian and steal it back.
- **60-second fun:** Grab, run and aim one Toss action; relay to a friend, fake a pass, or let a rival grab the loose gem before you steal it back and bank.
- **TikTok clip:** Your friend catches your “gift,” the gem screams their name, the keeper pivots, and you reclaim it at the gate.
- **Stands out:** The treasure is both the prize and the guardian's moving target; stealing becomes a social passing game rather than another Brainrot base or Egg route.
- **Smallest proof:** One gem, keeper, short arena, bank and toss/catch; reuse item identity/drop/secure and keeper cues; add trajectory, catch and target-follow behavior.

### 2. Steal the King's Bed — wild
- **Hook:** Steal a sleeping king's entire treasure-bed and surf it out before he wakes.
- **60-second fun:** Grab a handle immediately and pull quickly alone or with a friend; corners tip the king, collisions wake him, and the getaway becomes a short chase.
- **TikTok clip:** Two thieves sled a sleeping king through a doorway, his crown bounces, and he wakes just before the finish.
- **Stands out:** The funny guardian IS the cargo; a cooperative heist becomes an awkward moving comedy instead of carrying another egg-shaped collectible.
- **Smallest proof:** One bed, king, broad corner, ramp and bank; reuse cart presentation and keeper warning cues; handles, steerable momentum and wake behavior are new.

### 3. Trojan Treasure — wild / distinctive twist candidate
- **Hook:** Disguise as treasure so a rival carries YOU into their vault, then pop out and steal their round prize.
- **60-second fun:** Tap into a gold-statue disguise, wiggle to tempt pickup and reveal whenever you choose; an NPC curator provides a solo route.
- **TikTok clip:** A rival celebrates finding a rare statue, then it sprouts legs, grabs their trophy and runs.
- **Stands out:** Your opponent's greed becomes your transport; the thief is the stolen object, combining a treasure heist with human bait rather than reskinning a hatch loop.
- **Smallest proof:** Two rooms, one disguise, one round prize, carry/reveal and a simple NPC courier; reuse plot/carry donors; disguise, infiltration and host rules are new.

### 4. Roll Up the Vault
- **Hook:** Roll a sticky golden treasure ball through a vault and steal everything that sticks to it.
- **60-second fun:** Push through coins, chairs and crowns, grow enormous, scoop a rival's loose haul, then choose a narrow safe bank or a greedy oversized doorway.
- **TikTok clip:** A tiny ball becomes a giant junk sphere with the guard's hat attached, jams a doorway, then bursts into a visible haul and actual payout.
- **Stands out:** The growing physical pile IS progress; Katamari-like rolling is an acknowledged inspiration, while stealing contested loot and escaping replace Egg/Brainrot's carry-and-hatch pattern.
- **Smallest proof:** One room, six prop types, two exits and one ball per player; reuse room art and reward presentation; rolling, attachment, contested loose loot and size rules are new.

### 5. Granny's Golden Teeth
- **Hook:** Steal Granny's golden teeth and wear them to whistle her huge guard-dog away from your escape.
- **60-second fun:** Snatch the dentures, use Whistle to send the dog after a bouncing thrown bone, and escape Granny while the dog scatters a rival's loose loot.
- **TikTok clip:** A thief grins with enormous gold teeth as the dog bowls Granny into pillows and the stolen teeth escape.
- **Stands out:** The stolen treasure grants an immediately usable prank; the guardian reacts physically, rather than serving as a generic NPC behind another collectible reskin.
- **Smallest proof:** One yard, dentures, Granny, dog, bone and pillow pile; reuse keeper pursuit/tell cues; dog lure, whistle/bone interaction and prop reactions are new.

### 6. Cannonball Burglars
- **Hook:** Load yourself into a treasure-chest cannon, rob a vault on landing, and bounce the chest home.
- **60-second fun:** Drag to aim and fire immediately, grab a trophy, then choose a safe low return or risky roof bounce while rivals intercept loose chest loot.
- **TikTok clip:** A chest smashes through a museum roof, a bad bounce lands in a rival's bank, and the next launch steals the prize back.
- **Stands out:** Aiming and bouncing author the getaway; this is a thief's stunt playground rather than a prescribed walking lane or parry exam.
- **Smallest proof:** Two cannons, one vault, bounce roof, bank and treasure; reuse cave/cart flight presentation and carry/secure ideas; player aim, launch and bounce are new.

**Novelty honesty:** Trojan Treasure is the proposed “nobody else has this” twist to investigate: being the loot is how you infiltrate a rival. Prop disguise and Trojan-horse fiction already exist. Worldwide uniqueness is UNKNOWN; no world-first claim is justified. The novelty to prove is the actual human bait → transport → reveal → counter-heist interaction.

## Pick two, then prove one

For a transparent joint ordering, add each concept's ordinal FUN and STANDOUT positions across the three critics, weighting both criteria equally. Lower is better. These sums are **rank arithmetic, not measured fun scores**: Roll 14, Bed 15, Diamond 19, Trojan 21, Cannon 26, Teeth 31. All three critics' personal top-two pairs split between Roll, Bed and Diamond; the shared ordering resolves that disagreement without using cost.

**1. Roll Up the Vault.** Best first candidate if Zion likes a huge visible haul, physical greed and rivalry that works even without a coordinated friend. It gives an entertaining acquisition action before an economy exists. Its main threat is poor control feel, not a lack of later content.

**2. Steal the King's Bed.** Best candidate if Zion likes funny characters, friends shouting and cooperative disasters. It has the clearest absurd premise and a guardian involved in the theft itself. Its main threat is becoming a slow hauling chore. Additional proposal for a later proof: both contributing thieves share the banked team result; a helper must not merely wait for a turn at one exclusive prize.

Keep them separate. Do not combine the bed, rolling ball, screaming gem, pets and raid into a new feature pile. Diamond is the strongest alternative if Zion's answers prioritize direct stealing and betrayal over the two shortlisted fantasies.

| Later single proof, after Zion chooses | Compare side by side | What would justify continuing / what would kill it |
|---|---|---|
| Roll: one room, one satisfying small-to-huge haul and bank; a second player can take loose loot. | Steal an Egg's visible theft/chase; Grow a Garden's unmistakable exceptional object; Katamari-like rolling as the acknowledged mechanical inspiration. | Continue if collecting is pleasant before the payout and a second run invites a different greedy route. Cut/rework if steering is a fight, a door softlocks the haul, or only the first growth reveal is funny. |
| Bed: one quick solo drag, one two-person corner/ramp getaway, one readable wake. | Steal an Egg's wake-and-run clarity; Huss Valley's witnessed rescue/failure; our guardian-as-cargo inversion must create its own joke. | Continue if both players can cause and recover a funny mistake and want to swap roles. Cut/rework if the passenger watches, cooperation means waiting, or a crash merely resets everyone. |

The later acceptance evidence should include a cold solo attempt, a pair of friends and an unplanned second attempt; look for voluntary “again,” deliberate improvisation and clear accounts of what happened. No numerical acceptance score is invented here. A muted phone-sized recording should explain the action. Use one real focal treasure/guardian art direction before scaling; greybox only the core interaction first. Zion judges one complete moment against the reference, then decide whether more is warranted. **None of that future build or play work was performed in this pass.**

Reward proposal for those proofs: show the physical haul first, bank it, then reveal only its actual contents/value. Give a memorable recovered object a visible trophy place later if the loop earns it. Pets, collection rates and extra zones remain optional follow-on decisions; no fake payout, new currency or monetization plan is needed to decide whether stealing is enjoyable.

## Lessons

- When a loop is mostly avoidance, give the stolen object a usable behavior, because players need a way to author the surprise.
- When critics love a premise but rank its play last, test the repeated verb first, because novelty can hide waiting and weak agency.
- When a reference study starts mid-game, label first-session pacing UNKNOWN, because an impressive account is not onboarding evidence.
