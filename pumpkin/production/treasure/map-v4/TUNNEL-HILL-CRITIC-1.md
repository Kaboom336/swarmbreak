## Named aesthetic standards

Compared with **Minecraft caves**, the darkness, block language and glowing accents communicate an underground route, but the uniform corridor, panel ceiling and repeated upright wall strips lack the irregular volume and terrain continuity that make a cave feel excavated from land. Compared with **Zelda: Breath of the Wild grassy cave entrances**, the planted slope communicates the intended setting, but the rigid gateway, smooth green face and abrupt grass-to-rock relationship are less convincing as a weathered opening in a hillside. These are qualitative aesthetic comparisons, not exact reference matches.

## Three key flaws and corrections

1. **The hill and mouth look assembled.** Repeated exposed slope ribs, nearly parallel wall seams and the peaked stone lintel resemble architecture. Vary the slope breaks, bury rib ends into adjacent ground, blend the lower slope outward, and replace the lintel with an asymmetric rock arch whose stones continue into the surrounding hillside. Add visible moss patches where rock meets grass and a few irregular hanging vines.
2. **The interior does not sell natural enclosure or a warm destination.** Green ceiling tiles and tall stone strips make a straight hall; skylights do not produce readable light, and stacked rounded objects obstruct the far end. Make the ceiling a darker uneven rock underside, preserve two roof holes but give each a visible sky aperture and small daylight pool, and move the rounded objects away from the exit sightline. Establish a broad warm sunlit ground patch beyond the mouth, visible from the passage center.
3. **The crystal language is mostly glowing posts.** Give cyan crystals pointed faceted tips, unequal heights and slight outward angles. Reduce their repeated even spacing and light intensity so facets remain visible. Keep flat mushroom caps visually distinct from crystal clusters, and let a few clusters emerge from rock cracks rather than sit along a corridor border.

Lessons: When a hill uses repeated ribs, break and bury them because exposed repetition reads as construction. When an exit must guide the player, keep its warm ground and opening visible because a tiny yellow prop cannot carry the destination. When crystals glow, retain pointed facets because flat saturated bars lose the crystal silhouette.
