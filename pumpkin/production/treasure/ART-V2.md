## Style rules

1. Build the joke into the object: furniture stuck to a ball, king still in bed, diamond with a screaming face. No generic jewel with a rarity label as the only identity.
2. One focal treasure, one obvious route, one readable guardian reaction. Keep the camera center clear of text and decoration.
3. Thick silhouettes first: edges and handles should still read when a sheet is reduced to phone size. No thin filigree, noisy surface distress, intricate cloth or realistic skin.
4. Chamfered cuboids, cylinders, spheres and broad facets share an edge softness. Architecture has straight chunky masses; heroes have round comic accents. No world outlines.
5. Normal player camera: proposed 70° FOV, behind the avatar, zoomed out enough to expose steering space and doorway clearance; never an aerial dollhouse to hide poor routes. Redraw target: camera about 3 ch high, 4 ch behind, pitched down about 25°. Guardian turnaround and isolated toys are presentation exceptions. UI uses screen space over the player view. These are prompt targets, not measured camera calibration.
6. Daylight, including both interiors. Use windows and a pale ambient fill; no black corners, heavy fog, dramatic rim lighting or blur during action. Menu background defocus is acceptable only while choosing.
7. Gold remains a readable yellow surface with a broad highlight. Cream paths stay lighter than teal walls. Restrict accent colors to the focal toy, interaction or reaction.
8. A concept image is not a geometry specification. Match the silhouette and hierarchy; simplify tiny details during later production. Any mismatches listed by the critic remain visible in the audit.

## Do / don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Show actual collected objects on the sphere | Replace all haul with identical coins |
| Keep the sleeping king's face and handles visible | Hide the joke under particles or blankets |
| Enlarge crown, key, chair and doorway silhouettes | Add fine ornamental trim to imply quality |
| Put decoration in deliberate edge clusters | Fill the movement corridor with unrelated props |
| Keep timer and haul separate with one banner | Add competing objectives, shops or currencies |
| Use original models inspired by visual principles | Copy a reference game's characters or assets |

## Lessons

- When the treasure is the joke, show its behavior in the silhouette, because an attractive generic jewel cannot explain the heist.
- When a source image is promotional, pair it with gameplay frames, because thumbnail detail and lighting can overpromise the playable view.
- When planning phone art, cap geometry, clutter and effects separately, because simple textures alone do not establish performance.
