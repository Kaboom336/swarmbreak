## How a good Roblox game looks, plays and feels

### Art style and lighting

The collection's best Roblox examples are not the most detailed. They use one readable noun, chunky silhouettes, a small palette and a clear foreground/background value split. Office Warfare's neon city, the Halloween catch scene and the stronger bridge/VFX comparisons all make the focal object readable before detail. Pumpkin should stay toy-like and authored: broad ribs, a large stem/window, chunky graveyard/candy props, dark ink separation and six semantic colours. Cool blue/purple can own the environment while warm orange owns the pumpkin; magenta is danger/rare, lime is safe/success, cyan is speed/interactive and yellow is currency/reward. Brightness and bloom must be concentrated at state changes, not applied to the whole map.

### VFX and animation

VFXLoom, Haymaker, the microbot hand and the two attack comparisons all reward shaped, timed effects rather than volume. The reusable grammar is anticipation (100–250 ms), one clean contact frame, then a 300–700 ms directional result. Pumpkin needs countable crack ticks, falling rind chunks, seed/goo accents and a cosmetic burst that clears before the prize. Motion leads: rider brace/lean, stem lag, shell sag and detached chunks make the state readable; particles support those poses. Moon Animator is appropriate for short latch/reveal beats, while normal steering and collision remain responsive code-driven animation.

### UI, camera and packaging

Good UI in these posts uses center anchors, crisp transparent icons, thick dark strokes, a small number of card sizes, obvious close/next actions and filters only when collections need them. Bad AI UI exposes inconsistent padding, weak icon crops, corner-origin tweens and overlapping panels. Gameplay camera should hold the pumpkin, horizon and next decision; authored cameras are reserved for a sub-second launch commitment and the post-burst reward. The collection also treats thumbnails/trailers as part of the product: three honest variants and one short verb-led trailer should be tested, but must use real game renders and be judged with first-session retention, not clicks alone.

### How the assets should be made

- **Code-built parts:** track collision, deterministic lanes, greybox bridges, spawn logic, beacons and runtime effects. These need exact dimensions and tests more than mesh detail.
- **Store kits/templates:** temporary secondary props, mechanics prototypes and UI layout starting points only. Scan scripts/remotes, record licences, remove unused content, normalize the whole visual language and never let a template supply Pumpkin's identity.
- **Real modelling:** the hero pumpkin, Healthy/Cracked/Collapsed states, rind chunks, rider window, signature rewards, eggs/pets and focal landmarks belong in Blender or another deliberate modelling pipeline. Use stable pivots/names, measured triangles and baked/licensed textures.
- **AI/code-generated art:** useful for ideation, greybox structures, thumbnail composition tests and procedural VFX textures. It still needs human layout, palette, cleanup and gameplay-distance review. The collection repeatedly identifies obvious AI UI/models as a reason players leave.

The videos therefore strengthen the existing 80/20 split in `GOOD-GAME-STUDY.md`: generic environment vocabulary can come from reviewed kits, but the object/state journey and everything players stare at must be custom. They also add a production rule the study should make explicit: finish the map composition, hero silhouette and one complete screen before scaling logic/content, because a mechanically complete but visibly generated game is still rejected on sight.

## Support for or changes to GOOD-GAME-STUDY.md

- **Supported:** one giant readable object, visible state change, short repeatable verbs, immediate multi-channel feedback, controlled saturation, thick UI strokes, a stable chase camera and a clean reward pedestal all recur in the collection.
- **Supported:** the study's asset split is correct. Claude/code can erect structures and collision, kits can accelerate secondary dressing, and custom modelling must carry the hero shell, chunks, pets/rewards and focal landmarks.
- **Strengthened:** “custom” must include interface motion and icon production. Center anchors, clipping, padding, filters and crisp transparent PNGs need acceptance checks; a purchased or generated pack is not finished until it matches Pumpkin's tokens.
- **Strengthened:** packaging and analytics belong in the quality loop. Test three truthful thumbnails plus a short trailer, instrument the complete first-session funnel, and improve the game before spending on traffic.
- **Changed:** AI can be used more aggressively for prototypes—procedural flipbooks, GLB drafts, greybox structures, camera blocking and thumbnail layouts—but generated output is evidence only after human cleanup, rights/provenance review, performance limits and in-engine playtesting.
- **Changed:** add a solo-safe social experiment after the core slice, not before it. A ghost/friend target may help co-play, but the first roll, deterioration and reward cannot require populated servers.
- **Rejected:** the asset-ripping workflow and near-copy Etsy/game workflows shown in some posts are not production options. Pumpkin needs original or properly licensed inputs with recorded provenance.

### Zion's mechanic: roll → deteriorate → explode → reveal

No collection post shows this exact pumpkin sequence, so its mechanic remains Zion's direction rather than a recovered TikTok claim. The videos do validate how to execute it. Moon Animator supplies short authored commitment/reveal beats; Haymaker supplies anticipation/contact/result timing; the microbot and attack clips show directional fragments around one strong silhouette; and the chest/crate posts show why the screen must clear before the reward is named.

Keep the study's **10–14 s playable roll** and **2.1–2.5 s terminal sequence**. Warning begins while steering still matters; cracks become countable, saturation falls, authored shell chunks detach, the lower silhouette sags, control ends cleanly, then a custom non-damaging burst sends rind/seeds along travel. After a short quiet gap, the exact reward gets a pedestal/name/rarity shot and the player returns safely. Collision remains authoritative and stable through the visible deformation, no obstacle decision is placed in the first-run finale, and reduced-effects mode lowers particles without removing the three readable mesh states.
